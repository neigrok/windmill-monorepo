# Communication guide

How Windmill speaks. It holds for every word a person outside the build reads: product copy, public
pages, store listings, emails, release notes and posts. Read it before writing one.

Three neighbours rule what this guide does not. [Honesty](brand-foundations.md#honesty) rules what the
product may do and show, with the strings it never uses at a sign-in, a limit or a lost capability.
[The text budget](guidelines/text-budget.md) rules how many words an element may spend.
[The marketing strategy](../marketing/strategy.md) says where Windmill speaks, and to whom.

## The idea

**The idea.** Windmill is where a person keeps growing in body, skill and mind. Bring your own AI or
use ours at a fair price. Nothing is locked and nothing is graded.

**Why.** Machines can now do much of the work people do. In Windmill the AI gives clarity and
guidance, helps keep the record and helps a person past what blocks them. It does not do the work for
them: the growing stays the person's.

**The name.** A mill takes its time and grinds the grain into flour anyway. It takes nothing by
force. The grain is yours, and so is the bread.

**The mill, not the wind.** Windmill is where a person's growth is kept. The AI that helps can be
ours or their own. Their own is an assistant they connect through MCP, which reaches Roadmap and
Gym; Journal has no MCP door, and Windmill takes no provider key or token from a person.

**Respect.** People are not a problem to fix. Being human needs no proving. Windmill grades no one
and hurries no one.

**What we never say.** Nothing that frightens or belittles: no "AI will replace you", no "don't fall
behind", no "fix yourself". The AI never "works for you" or "does it for you": it guides, and the
person does the work. Journal is reflection, never therapy. One is a fair price for the AI and the
work, never "at cost".

## How the AI is described

[The idea](#the-idea) says what the AI does and what no string says of it. Two rules stand beside it:

- "Your AI, or ours" is one feature in two views: the assistant a person already has, connected to
  their account, or Windmill's own. Where a room has both, a page names both. Where a room has one,
  the page does not imply the other.
- "AI-native" is Windmill's word for itself: AI is part of each room, not an extra set beside it.

## Voice

- **Voice**: encouraging and a little playful, but not jokey. Copy stays domain-neutral — it
  works for a room makeover, a fitness goal, or a product launch alike.
- **Person**: second person ("your roadmap", "you unlock") in the product; first-person
  plural ("we") only in about/company copy. First-person singular has one place: the founder
  note, which [Channels](#channels) rules.
- **Casing**: sentence case everywhere — headings, buttons, nav. No ALL-CAPS except tiny
  badge/eyebrow labels, which use small-caps-style letter-spacing instead of visual shouting.
- **Emoji**: not used. The metaphor is carried by icons and glow, not emoji.
- **How much**: `guidelines/text-budget.md` is canon for how many words an element may spend. The
  short of it — budget the chrome, never the content; a permanent caption is a design failure wearing
  a sentence; a long true notice is *less* honest than a short true one, because almost nobody reads
  it; and **salience and concreteness beat brevity**, so make the one fact that matters impossible to
  miss before you start cutting.
- **Sentence length**: short. One idea per sentence in UI copy; marketing copy allows one
  longer sentence per paragraph, max two clauses.
- **Plain words**: one idea per heading and one description per page. A stranger with no context
  learns from the first screen what the thing is and what it does; an idea about it comes after.
- **The person acts**: the person is the subject of the sentence, and no sentence makes them the
  weaker party beside a machine.

## Names

- The brand is Windmill. A public page names each room in full at first mention, Windmill Roadmap,
  Windmill Journal and Windmill Gym, and names windmill.works, so a search does not confuse Windmill
  with the unrelated developer automation platform.
- In public a room is called what a stranger would search for: Windmill Roadmap is a skill tree,
  Windmill Journal is a daily journal and Windmill Gym is a workout log.
- Each room's own vocabulary, with the words it never uses, is in its
  [product principles](brand-foundations.md#product-principles).
- Windmill One is the paid line. [Accounts and sign-in](brand-foundations.md#accounts-and-sign-in) and
  [Landings](brand-foundations.md#landings) rule where it may be named and where its price may stand.

## Claims

- A sentence says only what a person can do today, on the surface it names.
- A condition is said in the sentence that makes the promise: "Once you have an account and twenty
  written pages".
- A number appears only when the code or a document that states it has been checked.
- Nothing is offered that cannot be had. Until an app has a public listing, a page says it is in beta
  and not public yet, and links no download or beta.
- Nothing hurries a person: [Numbers and money](brand-foundations.md#numbers-and-money).
- Before a string ships, find the line of code or the document that makes it true. If there is none,
  the string does not ship.

## Who speaks

- Windmill speaks as the product, and agents write and speak for the product.
- Nobody but the owner writes, sends or publishes the owner's own story, or anything signed as the
  owner.
- No page says who makes Windmill. A page says what the product does.
- The repository is public. A commit message, a pull request, a document and a roadmap node are read by
  strangers, and none carries anything personal about the owner or the people near them.

## Channels

- **Publish only when there is something to say.** There is no posting cadence, and nothing is made to keep activity. Every piece shows real work or a real idea.
- **Work follows readiness.** No campaign is timed to a season or a launch date. Observation windows are measurement, not deadlines.
- **Brand-led, with at most one founder note.** Writing and publishing it are the owner's alone, and no agent drafts it. If it is published, it is in the first person under a pseudonym, tells why Windmill exists and what the name means, appears once, and is posted only where disclosed self-posts are allowed. Public creative and posts do not name or show the founder; a post still discloses that its author builds Windmill.
- **A master is paid for their time, never their verdict.** [Masters](../marketing/reports/09-creator-campaign.md)
- **Every rule here holds in every channel**, with [Honesty](brand-foundations.md#honesty).
