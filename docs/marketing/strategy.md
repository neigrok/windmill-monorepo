# Windmill's first 90 days of marketing

**Lead public acquisition with Roadmap. Test Gym with lifters who already follow a program.** A stranger can open an animated skill tree on the web, try a step and make a plan of their own. That gives a community host something useful to share today. Gym has a different advantage worth testing: someone who brings their own routine has a reason to open their log at their next workout. A return to either product is the first outcome worth investing in.

The plan starts **November 12, 2026**. The proposed first-month budget is **$0 incremental cash and eight founder hours per week**. Keep the two pilots small enough to see where each person gets stuck. No public store release, checkout, sponsor, directory acceptance or minimum traffic forecast is assumed.

## Who we want to reach

| Priority | Person and moment | Promise to demonstrate | First destination and action |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 · Roadmap | A solo builder with a small project they mean to ship this month. Their checklist has tasks but gives them little help deciding what must happen first. | “Turn your release checklist into a skill tree. Finish one step and see what opens next.” | [Roadmap](https://windmill.works/roadmap). Choose **Ship v1** in the [starter picker](https://windmill.works/roadmap#/app/start), write the one-sentence promise for the project, then mark the step complete. |
| 2 · Gym | A lifter with a repeatable routine and two training days ahead. Their current record might be a note, a spreadsheet or another log. | “Bring the routine you already follow. Log it on your phone and see what you lifted when you come back.” | Arrange a supported native build. iOS uses TestFlight and Android has a signed APK. The [Gym web page](https://windmill.works/gym) explains the product. Workout entry is on the phone. |
| Later · Journal | A writer who wants to find a thought from an earlier day. | “Write a page today. Find it again when you need it.” | [Journal](https://windmill.works/journal). Test manual writing and search once a pilot slot opens. |

Use **Windmill Roadmap**, **Windmill Gym** or **Windmill Journal** and the domain **windmill.works** in a first mention. A person searching for “Windmill MCP” can otherwise land on a developer automation platform. For assistant users, describe a concrete job such as “show me the steps I can work on next.” Link to the [connection guide](https://windmill.works/connect.html). Lead with one product per invitation. Explain the shared account when somebody wants a second product.

Recruit in English from groups the founder or a host can actually reach. Start with one group per product where possible. Record the platform and recruitment source. If the next cohort comes from a different market or a January fitness push, keep its result separate.

## The first seven days

| Date | Owner and time | Deliverable | How to check it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nov 12–13 | Founder + engineering, 1 founder hour | Baseline card with the public search inventory, property access, and the last complete week of available funnel counts. Record unknown fields as unknown. | One unassisted dry run of each pilot task on its intended surface. Log where sign-in or installation interrupts it. [Scorecard](funnels.md) |
| Nov 13–15 | Founder, 2 hours | One 30-second screen recording of the original Ship v1 starter. One native Gym clip made with invented workout data. | The Roadmap clip shows the promise being completed and what unlocks. The Gym clip shows the previous session alongside the next log. Each ends with one action and the real destination. |
| Nov 14–17 | Founder, 3 hours | Ask two builder-group hosts for an opt-in feedback slot and two lifting-group or club organizers for testers who repeat a routine. Use existing contacts or the group's designated feedback thread. Start a private invitation tally. | Count invitations, opt-ins and first attempts separately by source. A host forwarding a link counts as one placement. Never claim to know how many people saw it. |
| Nov 17–18 | Founder, 2 hours | Observe the first attempts, remove one recruiting or explanation obstacle, and schedule time for the second observation. | A tester can explain what to do next in their own words. Mark anyone who needed live coaching. Keep the outcome in the denominator. |

**Builder invitation.** “I make Windmill Roadmap. If you're shipping a small project, would you try the Ship v1 tree with your own project? The first step is writing its one-sentence promise. I'd like to know whether you can tell what to do next. You can try it in your browser.” Send the Roadmap link after a person opts in or where the host permits it.

**Lifter invitation.** “I make Windmill Gym. I'm looking for people who already have a routine and train twice in the next week. Would you use our phone log for those two sessions and tell me what you chose to use for the second one? You can keep the exercises, weights and notes to yourself.” Confirm the install route before inviting someone.

**At follow-up**, ask “What did you actually use the next time?” and “Where did you stop?” A volunteered reason such as “I couldn't find my last set” informs the next brief. Record a bounded reason category and the count. Keep personal stories out of analytics and public copy. Obtain separate permission for any testimonial. Never trade an incentive for a positive review.

## Weeks two through thirteen

| Window | Work and decision |
|---|---|

With permission, observe five first attempts per product. Keep creative and destination fixed long enough to interpret the results. If intake stalls, report the shortfall. Rework if fewer than four activate or fewer than two return. For **Gym**, continue with **at least 4/10** completing two sessions of at least five sets in seven days. Rework at **0–1/10**. Intermediate counts call for interviews and another same-size cohort. Read the full Roadmap result on December 9 for the November 25 cutoff. A later signup enters the next intake. | **Dec 10–Jan 10** | For a passing Roadmap pilot, repeat the same starter with a second builder community. Give its host the clip, a playable link and one question for feedback. Ask returning lifters to show where planning or changing their routine cost effort. A failed pilot gets one revised first-use test before broader distribution. |
| **Jan 11–Feb 9** | Choose one channel for the next month using returning people per founder hour, unaided completion and the second cohort's result. Expand one variable: another original starter, another partner or one directory listing. If neither pilot repeats, spend the next cycle on the observed product obstacle. The first-month numbers are proposed decision rules for learning with a small group. Count sign-ups, refusals and missing follow-ups. Do not estimate market size or a conversion rate for cold traffic from these people. [Definitions and weekly review](funnels.md)

## How distribution can compound

- **Roadmap through hosts.** Give a maker-group organizer a link members can open during a build session, one original starter and a 30-second example. A participant can return to the same tree and explicitly share or publish their own. After the pilot passes, ask the host whether they would run it with another group. Count their independently recruited starters and returns. [Roadmap pilot](reports/11-roadmap-seed-trees.md)
- **Search and AI answers.** Start with the six public destinations for the brand, three products, gallery and agent setup. Use inspection reports to establish indexing. Put editorial time into the page where a person can carry out the intent. One good tree with crawlable steps is worth testing before producing a collection. [Search runbook](reports/06-brand-search-identity.md)
- **Assistants.** A builder may already ask Claude, Cursor or Codex to plan a project. Windmill can give that plan a home with prerequisites and progress. First test whether the person can connect with the requested grant, get a useful result, open it on the web and resume it later. Apply to one directory after that path works for strangers. [Assistant distribution](reports/12-directories-and-show-hn.md)
- **Gym through training partners.** The strongest introduction is another lifter running the same program. Ask a returning tester if they would recommend the log to one training partner. Measure whether the partner starts and returns. A share card or creator clip earns engineering or media time after that behavior appears. [Gym pilot](reports/08-gym-demand-cohort.md)
- **Journal through existing interest.** Offer manual writing and retrieval to volunteers who already journal. The test reads days written and whether they find an earlier page. Publishing a user's writing plays no role in distribution. [Journal brief](reports/13-journal-voice-cohort.md)

## When money enters the plan

Windmill One checkout is closed. Manual work is free. A request for an invite, use of an external assistant, a free hosted Coach turn or a hypothetical price answer is evidence of interest. **A paid-demand test starts only when a customer can really buy**, the owner opens the paid line and approves funding, billing and refund behavior is verified, and hosted AI costs are measured. Customer-facing claims about AI must match the enabled allowance. [Billing and usage contract](../AI_USAGE_REVIEW.md)

For a later controlled pilot, retain **$12/month as a price hypothesis**. Invite 50 qualified lifters to a working offer. Proposed learning gate: five genuine non-refunded buyers and at least half of those buyers performing a second useful Coach task in a later week. Keep an unobserved payer window pending. If this gate passes, price a single creator test with usage rights, a cash ceiling and a working store destination. Each commercial commitment is an owner decision.

For scale, retain the stricter gate: 50 unique non-refunded payers across successive cohorts from the same repeatable channel, at least 70% of eligible first renewals kept, and fully loaded acquisition cost at or below realised 90-day contribution. Include founder time, content and allocated free AI use. Any bounded learning spend before those cohorts mature needs its own owner-approved cap.
