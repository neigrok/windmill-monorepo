# 10 · Test whether a voluntary recap brings people back

**Deferred lifecycle experiment.** Use only after a core product has returning users, a free experiment
slot, a verified return measure and enough people who have explicitly opted in to learn from a comparison.
The two free pilots measure spontaneous repeat use. Research follow-ups belong after each observation
window.

**Proposed first version.** Test one product, one factual count and one relevant destination. For example, a
Gym user who chooses a weekly recap could receive the number of sessions logged and a link to history.
Preview the exact email at opt-in so the recipient knows what can appear in an inbox. Leave it off by
default. Keep workout names, movement names, journal text and other personal content out of subject lines,
delivery logs and event properties. Verify suppression, unsubscribe and failure handling before sending.

**Owner's test sheet**

1. Define the eligible population, delivery cadence, event that counts as return and minimum lift worth
   maintaining the email for. Use the observed baseline to calculate the required sample and duration before
   allocating a slot. Keep the experiment in backlog if the needed sample is out of reach.
2. Randomly assign consenting people to a recap or a no-send holdout. Compare next-week product returns over
   **all assigned people** in each arm. Report group sizes, counts, rates and their uncertainty. Opens and
   clicks diagnose delivery and routing.
3. Stop on a consent or suppression error. If the groups are too small to resolve the planned effect, report
   an inconclusive result. Retain the program only if return improves enough to justify delivery and support
   costs and complaints remain within the predeclared limit.

A new settings control or email design needs owner-approved design work, and any delivery spend needs a
separate budget decision. [Measurement and maturity rules](../funnels.md)
