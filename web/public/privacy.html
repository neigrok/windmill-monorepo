Privacy

Updated August 2026

The short version: signed out, your trees and journal pages live on your device — the one exception is text you paste into the birth canvas and ask us to shape. Signing in syncs them to your account so they follow you — and that’s the main reason we store anything.

What we store

  • Signed out: your trees and your journal pages stay in this browser, on this device. The one thing that leaves is text you paste into the birth canvas and ask us to shape into a tree: that goes to us and on to Anthropic, because shaping it is the thing you asked for. What we do keep is anonymous usage events under a random per-browser key, so we can tell what’s working. No names, no content.
  • Signed in: your email address; the trees you sync; the journal pages you sync, the passages they’re cut into and the vectors we compute from those to find echoes; your training log — every set, session, routine and movement you add, plus any coach link you’ve minted; your active sessions; and any AI-tool connections you’ve granted — plus those same usage events, now tied to your account. That’s the list we hold. Some of it also travels: your journal pages go to an outside company as you write them, and who processes it with us says exactly what goes where.

Signing in

Windmill uses magic links — and, in the apps, one-time emailed codes — there are no passwords, so there is nothing to leak or reuse. Each link or code works once and lasts 15 minutes. Sessions last 90 days per device; see and revoke every one, or sign out everywhere, from account settings.

Email we send

One kind: the sign-in you ask for — a link, or in the apps a one-time code — including the variant that plants your copy of a shared tree. That’s every email we send today. We don’t send marketing you didn’t ask for.

Sharing

A share link makes that tree’s page visible to anyone holding the link, and anyone holding it can fork their own copy — progress cleared.

Measurement

We look at product usage in aggregate to understand what’s working. No ad trackers, no selling data, no third-party profiles.

Who processes it with us

A few outside companies each handle a specific job, and only ever see the data that job needs:

  • Paddle — our seller of record for Windmill One. If you subscribe, Paddle takes the payment and handles tax; your billing details go to them, and we never see your card.
  • Resend — delivers the sign-in emails you ask for. It receives your email address to send the link or code, and nothing else.
  • Anthropic — the AI in all three rooms, and they do not work alike. In the roadmap it powers tending, which plants and reshapes a tree: when you tend, that tree and your instruction are sent to Anthropic to generate the edit — only when you ask for one. In the journal it is how echoes are found, and there you don’t ask. Saving a page whose text changed sends that text to Anthropic to be cut into passages; then passages from that page and from your earlier pages, each with the day it was written, are sent to judge which pairs are about the same thing. That happens on its own, usually a few seconds after you save — and again later if we change how the pipeline works, which re-reads pages you have not touched. In the gym, asking the coach a question sends that question and your training log — again only when you ask.
  • OpenAI — turns a journal Talk recording into text. When you use Talk, the audio of that take is sent to OpenAI to be transcribed; nothing else in the journal goes there, and we do not keep the recording. Talk is part of Windmill One, so it only happens on an account that has it.

The matching itself stays with us: the vectors behind echoes are computed on our own server and never leave it, and the search you run in the journal builds its own vectors — the model, the index and your query — on your device. None of them profile you, and none of them receive your data to sell it on.

Your data is portable

Export every tree as a zip of Markdown from account settings — the same format you can paste back to replant. Closing your account there signs out every device and cuts off every connected tool immediately, and it closes the whole account rather than one room. What it does not do is erase: the copies you’ve synced stay on our servers, and signing in again is the undo — at any time, bringing the account back whole. Trees on your devices always stay yours.
